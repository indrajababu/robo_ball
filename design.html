Design

The CADed end-effector and the ROS nodes and controllers that drive the robot end-to-end.

End-effector

The paddle is a custom part we modeled in CAD to mate with the arm's tool flange. It's sized and shaped for a predictable contact patch with the ball, with mounting features that stay rigid through repeated bounces.

Geometry

Why we picked this paddle face — size, curvature, and the contact surface that keeps the bounce repeatable.

Mount

The bolt pattern and adapter ring that mate the paddle to the wrist flange.

Iterations

How the design evolved across test runs — what failed, what we tightened up, and what we'd change next.

Software architecture

The juggling stack is split into ROS nodes that each own one job, connected by topics and services. Keeping the boundaries clean meant we could swap a controller or retune a predictor without touching the rest of the pipeline.

Nodes

  • Perception node — ingests camera frames and publishes ball position.
  • Trajectory predictor — fits a projectile model to recent positions and publishes a predicted catch point and time.
  • Planner — generates a joint-space trajectory from the current pose to the catch point.
  • Bringup / state machine — coordinates startup, calibration, and the bounce-and-catch loop.

Controllers

  • Joint controller — low-level position/velocity loop and the gains we landed on.
  • Trajectory tracking — how planned waypoints are fed to the joint controller.
  • Safety — joint limits, velocity caps, and the e-stop path.
